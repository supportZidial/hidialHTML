How to Reduce Failed Calls and Improve Answer Rates

A failed call and an unanswered call are different outcomes.

A failed call and an unanswered call are different outcomes. One may point to configuration or carrier handling; the other may reflect timing, customer expectations, or caller presentation. Separate those outcomes before changing the campaign.

Read SIP responses in context

Inspect the response together with the call sequence and the provider's records. SIP 486 means Busy Here. SIP 487 means Request Terminated, commonly following cancellation of a pending request. Neither code proves network congestion or spam blocking by itself.

A 503 response indicates service unavailability, while 480 indicates temporary unavailability. Authentication, routing, endpoint state, capacity, or policy can influence the responses your system receives. Ask your provider to interpret the specific call rather than treating one code as a complete diagnosis.

Save the timestamp and timezone, calling and called numbers, SIP Call-ID, destination, and failure description. These details help connect your dialer logs with the carrier's records.

Validate the route and number format

Confirm authentication, authorized source IPs, destination permissions, and the required number format. Check the caller ID you are allowed to present. Test across representative destination networks instead of relying on one successful call.

If signaling succeeds but audio fails, review the media path separately. Codecs, firewalls, RTP handling, and NAT can affect two-way audio without preventing SIP setup.

Improve contact-list quality

Remove invalid and disconnected numbers and review the source of the contacts. Distinguish technical validation from permission to call. Keep opt-out records synchronized across systems.

A number-lookup service can provide useful information, but coverage and accuracy vary. Check the result against your use case and do not assume one lookup detects every unavailable number or predicts whether someone will answer.

Match the dialer to available agents

An answered call still fails the customer if no representative is ready. Review dialing pace, connection delays, abandoned calls, and answering-machine detection. Reduce traffic when agent availability cannot support it.

Applicable abandonment requirements depend on the campaign and rules governing it. A single percentage is not a complete compliance checklist; confirm the measurement method and other conditions relevant to your operation.

Review answer rates separately

Compare answer rates by campaign, destination, lead age, and permitted calling window. Review caller-name presentation and reported warnings, but do not assume STIR/SHAKEN authentication guarantees an unlabeled call.

Make callbacks work, identify the business clearly, and avoid repeated attempts that ignore the customer's expectations or opt-out requests. Test one operational change at a time and compare against a documented baseline.
